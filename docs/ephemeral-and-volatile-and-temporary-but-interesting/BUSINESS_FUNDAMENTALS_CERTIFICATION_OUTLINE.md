# Business Fundamentals for New Entrepreneurs — Certification Outline

**Proposed ID:** `business-fundamentals-mba`  
**Proposed title:** Business Fundamentals for New Entrepreneurs  
**Proposed description:** You have mastered your craft — whether as a chef, electrician, something in the field of IT, or artist. Now you want to open your own business in Switzerland. This certification gives you the knowledge you need to run your company legally and successfully: Swiss company law, accounting obligations, tax requirements, employment rules, and the fundamentals of strategy, finance, marketing, and operations. Everything a Geschäftsführer / gérant d'entreprise needs to know, in language that makes sense.

**Important Notes:** 
- This certification is specifically designed for **starting and running a business in Switzerland**. All legal requirements, tax regulations, and business rules covered are Swiss-specific. While many concepts apply broadly, the compliance details are tailored to Swiss law and practice. Terms are provided in German and French, **IN BRACKETS** to serve both language regions.
- This certification **MUST BE** for young people so it must be written in a style that keeps the reader interested. Use anecdotes and small stories to do that.

---

## Overview

This certification is aimed at:

- Tradespeople and craftspeople (carpenters, electricians, plumbers, mechanics) who want to open their own business
- IT professionals (developers, system administrators, network engineers) starting their own consulting or service business
- Creative professionals (designers, photographers, artists) starting their own studio or agency
- Hospitality workers (chefs, bakers, hoteliers) opening their own establishment
- Anyone with technical or vocational expertise who now needs to run the business side

It assumes **no prior business education**. Every concept is introduced from first principles with practical examples from Swiss small business life. There are no complex formulas or academic theory without real-world application.

## Proposed Pages (Steps)

### 1. Introduction — From Craftsman to Geschäftsführer / gérant

Sets the stage: what it means to move from employee to business owner in Switzerland. Introduces the reality that technical skill is necessary but not sufficient — you now need to understand accounting, law, taxes, people management, and strategy. Uses the story of a real Swiss carpenter who nearly lost his business because he ignored bookkeeping until the Steueramt / l'office fiscal came knocking.

**Key concepts:** Gewerbebetrieb / entreprise commerciale, Geschäftsführer / gérant d'entreprise, the three hats you wear (operator, manager, owner), why business skills matter as much as craft skills

---

### 2. Choosing Your Business Structure — Einzelfirma / raison individuelle, GmbH / Sàrl, or AG / SA?

The first major decision every Swiss entrepreneur faces: what legal form should your business take? Covers Einzelfirma / raison individuelle (sole proprietorship), Gesellschaft mit beschränkter Haftung (GmbH) / Société à responsabilité limitée (Sàrl), and Aktiengesellschaft (AG) / Société anonyme (SA) in depth. Explains liability, capital requirements, formation costs, administrative burden, and tax implications. Includes a decision framework based on your risk tolerance, expected revenue, and growth plans.

**Key concepts:** Einzelfirma / raison individuelle, GmbH / Sàrl, AG / SA, Handelsregister / Registre du commerce, Stammkapital / capital social, Haftung / responsabilité, Firmenbuch / Registre du commerce, Notar / notaire, Verbandsregister / Registre des associations

---

### 3. Registering Your Business — The Official Path

Step-by-step walkthrough of registering a business in Switzerland. Covers the Gewerbebewilligung / autorisation de commerce (trade licence) from the Kanton / canton, entry in the Handelsregister / Registre du commerce (for GmbH/Sàrl/AG/SA), registration with the AHV/AVS/AI/APG (social security), and the SVA / AC (compensation fund). Explains what happens if you operate without proper registration — including the fines and back-payment liabilities.

**Key concepts:** Gewerbebewilligung / autorisation de commerce, Handelsregister / Registre du commerce, AHV-Meldung / déclaration AVS, SVA / AC (caisse de compensation), UID-Nummer / numéro IDE, Betreibungsamt / Office des poursuites, Nachzahlung / paiement rétroactif

---

### 4. Swiss Company Law — What Directors Must Know

Swiss law places specific obligations on Geschäftsführer / gérants and Verwaltungsräte / conseils d'administration. This page covers the duties of care and loyalty (Sorgfalts- und Treuepflicht / devoir de diligence et de loyauté), the prohibition on competition (Konkurrenzverbot / interdiction de concurrence), the duty to file for bankruptcy if over-indebted (Überschuldung / surendettement), and personal liability for unpaid social security contributions and withholding taxes. Uses the cautionary tale of a restaurant owner who became personally liable for 200,000 CHF in unpaid VAT / TVA.

**Key concepts:** OR (Obligationenrecht / Code des obligations), Art. 754 OR / Art. 754 CO, Überschuldungspflicht / obligation de déclaration de surendettement, Konkurrenzverbot / interdiction de concurrence, Geschäftsführerhaftung / responsabilité du gérant, Betreibungsverzug / retard de poursuites

---

### 5. The Swiss VAT System — Mehrwertsteuer / TVA Demystified

Everything you need to know about MWST / TVA (Taxe sur la Valeur Ajoutée): when you must register (the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold), the difference between standard (8.1%), reduced (2.6%), and special (3.8%) rates, how input tax deduction works, and the filing obligations (quarterly or semi-annual). Includes a worked example of a typical quarterly VAT return for a small services business.

**Key concepts:** MWST / TVA, MwSt-Nummer / numéro de TVA, Steuersatz / taux d'imposition, Vorsteuerabzug / déduction de l'impôt préalable, Effektivbesteuerung / taxation effective, Saldo-Methode / méthode du solde, ESTV / AFC (Administration fédérale des contributions)

---

### 6. Swiss Accounting Requirements — More Than Just Bookkeeping

Swiss law requires businesses to keep proper books (ordentliche Buchführung / comptabilité régulière). Covers the statutory requirements under the OR / CO, the difference between Einführung / tenue simple (single-entry) and doppelte Buchführung / comptabilité en partie double (double-entry), what records must be kept and for how long (10 years), and the requirement to produce annual financial statements. Explains when you need a Revision / révision (audit) — the CHF 20 million / 200 employees threshold.

**Key concepts:** OR 957ff / CO 957ss, ordentliche Buchführung / comptabilité régulière, Erfolgsrechnung / compte de résultat, Bilanz / bilan, Kontenrahmen / plan comptable, Revision / révision, Treuhand / fiduciaire

---

### 7. Understanding Financial Statements — Reading the Story of Your Business

How to read and understand the three key financial statements every Swiss business produces: the Erfolgsrechnung / compte de résultat (profit and loss), the Bilanz / bilan (balance sheet), and the Cashflow-Rechnung / calcul des flux de trésorerie (cash flow statement). Explains key ratios that matter: liquidity ratios, profitability margins, and working capital. Uses the example of a failing bakery whose owner did not understand that profit on paper does not mean cash in the bank.

**Key concepts:** Erfolgsrechnung / compte de résultat, Bilanz / bilan, Eigenkapital / capitaux propres, Fremdkapital / capitaux étrangers, Deckungsbeitrag / contribution à la couverture, Working Capital / fonds de roulement, Liquiditätsgrad / degré de liquidité

---

### 8. Taxation for Swiss Businesses — Federal, Cantonal, and Municipal

Switzerland's multi-layer tax system explained: direct federal tax (DBG / IFD), cantonal and municipal taxes (Staats- und Gemeindesteuern / impôts cantonaux et communaux), and how they apply to businesses. Covers the Gewinnsteuer / impôt sur le bénéfice (profit tax), Kapitalsteuer / impôt sur le capital (capital tax for GmbH/Sàrl/AG/SA), and the flat-rate taxation available to some sole proprietors. Explains tax deductibility of expenses, depreciation rules, and the importance of tax planning before year-end.

**Key concepts:** DBG / IFD (impôt fédéral direct), Staats- und Gemeindesteuern / impôts cantonaux et communaux, Gewinnsteuer / impôt sur le bénéfice, Kapitalsteuer / impôt sur le capital, Säule 3a / pilier 3a, Abschreibung / amortissement, Betriebsaufwand / charges d'exploitation

---

### 9. Employment Law in Switzerland — Hiring Your First Employee

The moment you hire your first employee, a complex web of obligations begins. Covers the Swiss employment contract (Arbeitsvertrag / contrat de travail), mandatory provisions (Urlaub / congés, Arbeitzeit / temps de travail, Kündigungsfristen / délais de congé), the obligation to register with the SVA / AC, salary payment rules (the quarterly rule), and the requirement to join a Pensionskasse / caisse de pension (occupational pension) once salaries exceed the entry threshold. Explains the difference between angestellt and arbeitnehmend (both meaning employed) and why it matters.

**Key concepts:** Arbeitsvertrag / contrat de travail, OR 319ff / CO 319ss, Lohnbuchhaltung / comptabilité de salaires, Quellensteuer / impôt à la source, BVG / LPP (Loi sur la prévoyance professionnelle), Pensionskasse / caisse de pension, Kündigungsfrist / délai de congé, Zeugnis / certificat de travail

---

### 10. Social Security and Payroll — The AHV/IV/EO / AVS/AI/APG System

Every Swiss employer becomes a mini-social-security administrator. This page covers the complete AHV/AVS/IV/AI/EO/APG system: employee and employer contribution rates (currently 5.3% each for AHV/AVS/IV/AI/EO/APG, plus 1.1% each for ALV/AC), the BVG / LPP pension obligations, the KTG / AJ (daily sickness allowance) insurance requirement, and the Unfallversicherung / assurance-accidents (UVG / LAA) obligation. Includes a worked example of calculating total employer cost for a CHF 5,000 monthly salary.

**Key concepts:** AHV / AVS (assurance-vieillesse et survivants), IV / AI (assurance-invalidité), EO / APG (allocations pour perte de gain), ALV / AC (assurance-chômage), BVG / LPP (Loi sur la prévoyance professionnelle), KTG / AJ (assurance-maladie journalière), UVG / LAA (Loi sur l'assurance-accidents), Lohnabrechnung / bulletin de salaire, Sozialabzüge / cotisations sociales, Arbeitgeberbeitrag / cotisation employeur

### 11. Business Planning — Writing a Plan That Actually Helps

Forget the 40-page business plan nobody reads. This page covers the lean business plan: the value proposition, customer segments, revenue model, cost structure, and the crucial break-even analysis. Focuses on the Swiss context: realistic revenue projections, typical cost structures for Swiss small businesses, and the importance of planning for seasonal fluctuations (think tourism or construction).

**Key concepts:** Business Model Canvas, Deckungsbeitrag / contribution à la couverture, Break-Even-Punkt / seuil de rentabilité, Umsatzplanung / planification des recettes, Kostenstruktur / structure des coûts, saisonale Schwankungen / fluctuations saisonnières

### 12. Pricing Strategy — What Should You Charge?

Pricing is where many Swiss small businesses fail. Covers cost-plus pricing, value-based pricing, and competitive pricing. Explains the importance of calculating your true cost including your own salary, social charges, and overhead. Includes the story of a web designer who charged too little and worked 60-hour weeks for less than minimum wage. Covers Swiss pricing psychology and when to publish prices.

**Key concepts:** Kostenvoranschlag / devis, Deckungsbeitrag / contribution à la couverture, Stundensatz / taux horaire, Tagessatz / taux journalier, Margenkalkulation / calcul de marge, Wertbasierte Preisgestaltung / tarification basée sur la valeur

---

### 13. Marketing for Small Swiss Businesses — Beyond Instagram

Marketing for a Swiss small business is different from global brands. Covers the essentials: defining your target customer (the Swiss love precision / les Suisses aiment la précision), building a local reputation, the role of word-of-mouth, the importance of being found on Google Maps, local advertising, and the realistic role of social media. Explains the Swiss resistance to aggressive sales tactics and the value of building long-term relationships.

**Key concepts:** Zielgruppe / groupe cible, Positionierung / positionnement, Google Business Profil / profil Google Business, Mund-zu-Mund-Propaganda / bouche à oreille, Empfehlungsmarketing / marketing par recommandation, lokale Werbung / publicité locale

---

### 14. Sales and Customer Relationships — The Swiss Way

The Swiss business culture values punctuality / ponctualité, preparation / préparation, and long-term thinking / réflexion à long terme. This page covers the sales process for B2B services, the importance of written quotations (Offerte / offre), the role of the contract, and managing customer expectations. Explains the Swiss Nein-saying / culture du "non" culture and how to interpret it. Covers handling complaints (Reklamationen / réclamations) and the importance of follow-through.

**Key concepts:** Verkaufsgespräch / entretien de vente, Offerte / offre, Kostenvoranschlag / devis, Auftragsbestätigung / confirmation de commande, Reklamation / réclamation, Kundenbeziehung / relation client, Follow-up / suivi

---

### 15. Managing Cash Flow — The Lifeblood of Your Business

More Swiss small businesses fail from cash flow problems than from lack of profit. Covers the cash conversion cycle, managing accounts receivable (the Swiss often pay late), the danger of overtrading, and building a cash reserve. Explains payment terms, reminder letters (Mahnungen / rappels), and the Betreibungsamt / Office des poursuites process. Includes the story of a successful contractor who went bankrupt because one major client paid 90 days late.

**Key concepts:** Liquidität / liquidité, Cashflow / flux de trésorerie, Zahlungsziel / délai de paiement, Mahnung / rappel, Betreibung / poursuite, Debitorenmanagement / gestion des débiteurs, Übertragung / cession

---

### 16. Financing Your Business — From Bootstrap to Credit

How to fund your Swiss business: personal savings (the most common source), Familie and friends / famille et amis, bank loans (the classic approach), leasing, government support programmes in Switzerland, and crowdfunding. Explains what Swiss banks want to see (a solid business plan, collateral / sûreté, and personal investment), and the concept of Eigenkapitalunterlegung / constitution de capitaux propres. Covers the risks of personal guarantees / cautions personnelles and using credit cards for business funding.

**Key concepts:** Eigenfinanzierung / autofinancement, Fremdfinanzierung / financement extérieur, Bankkredit / crédit bancaire, Leasing / crédit-bail, Bürgschaft / cautionnement, Eigenkapitalquote / ratio de capitaux propres, Kreditwürdigkeit / solvabilité

---

### 17. Insurance for Swiss Businesses — Protecting What You Built

Swiss businesses face specific risks that require insurance. Covers the mandatory insurances (Unfallversicherung / assurance-accidents for employees) and the essential voluntary ones: Betriebshaftpflicht / responsabilité civile d'entreprise (public liability), Sachversicherung / assurance de choses (property/equipment), Berufshaftpflicht / responsabilité civile professionnelle (professional liability), Rechtsschutz / protection juridique (legal protection), and Betriebsunterbrechung / perte d'exploitation (business interruption). Explains D&O / assurance D&O insurance for GmbH/Sàrl/AG/SA directors.

**Key concepts:** Betriebshaftpflicht / responsabilité civile d'entreprise, Berufshaftpflicht / responsabilité civile professionnelle, Sachversicherung / assurance de choses, Rechtsschutz / protection juridique, Betriebsunterbrechung / perte d'exploitation, D&O / assurance D&O (directors & officers), Prämie / prime

---

### 18. Contracts and Legal Agreements — Getting It in Writing

The Swiss value clear agreements / Les Suisses apprécient les accords clairs. Covers the essential contracts every business needs: General Terms and Conditions (AGB / CGV - Conditions Générales de Vente), service agreements / contrats de prestation de services, supply contracts / contrats de fourniture, lease agreements (for business premises) / baux commerciaux, and employment contracts / contrats de travail. Explains when a written contract is required by law, when verbal agreements are binding (more often than you think), and the importance of jurisdiction clauses / clauses d'élection de for.

**Key concepts:** Vertrag / contrat, AGB / CGV (Conditions Générales de Vente), Leistungsverzeichnis / cahier des charges, Gerichtsstand / élection de for, Schlichtung / conciliation, Vertragsarten / types de contrats, OR 1ff / CO 1ss

---

### 19. Intellectual Property — Protecting Your Ideas

Your brand, your methods, and your creations have value. Covers Swiss trademark registration (Schweizerisches Markenregister / Registre suisse des marques), copyright protection / droit d'auteur (automatic in Switzerland), patent basics / brevets, and the protection of trade secrets (Geschäftsgeheimnisse / secrets d'affaires). Explains when to register a Markenname / nom de marque, how to handle infringement / contrefaçon, and the difference between a Geschmacksmuster / modèle d'utilité and a patent / brevet.

**Key concepts:** Markenrecht / droit des marques, Urheberrecht / droit d'auteur, Patent / brevet, Geschmacksmuster / modèle d'utilité, Markenschutz / protection de la marque, IGE / IPI (Institut de la propriété intellectuelle)

---

### 20. Managing Operations and Quality — Delivering Consistently

Running the day-to-day operations of a Swiss business requires systems and standards. Covers process documentation / documentation des processus, quality control / contrôle qualité, supplier management / gestion des fournisseurs, inventory management / gestion des stocks, and the basics of Lean principles adapted for small businesses. Explains the Swiss emphasis on Pünktlichkeit / ponctualité and Qualität / qualité and how operational excellence builds reputation.

**Key concepts:** Prozessdokumentation / documentation des processus, Qualitätsmanagement / management de la qualité, Lieferantenbewertung / évaluation des fournisseurs, Lagerhaltung / gestion des stocks, Lean / Lean, Standardarbeit / travail standardisé

### 21. Growth and Scaling — When and How to Expand

Not every business should grow, but if you choose to, do it wisely. Covers the signs that you are ready to grow, the risks of growing too fast, hiring your first employees (the transition from solo to team), opening additional locations / succursales, and franchising. Explains the Swiss concept of Qualität vor Quantität / qualité avant quantité and the dangers of overextension.

**Key concepts:** Wachstum / croissance, Skalierung / mise à l'échelle, Expansion / expansion, Qualität vor Quantität / qualité avant quantité, Kapazitätsplanung / planification des capacités, Zweigstelle / succursale

### 22. Exit Strategies — Selling, Closing, or Succession

Every business journey ends eventually. Covers selling your business (Verkauf / vente), closing it (Liquidation / liquidation), and succession planning (Nachfolge / succession). Explains business valuation basics, the importance of clean books for a sale, the emotional aspects of letting go, and the legal requirements for properly dissolving a GmbH/Sàrl or AG/SA.

**Key concepts:** Unternehmensnachfolge / succession d'entreprise, Verkauf / vente, Liquidation / liquidation, Auflösung / dissolution, Firmenwert / valeur d'entreprise, Due Diligence / due diligence, Handelsregisterauszug / extrait du Registre du commerce

### 23. Professional Support — When to Call the Experts

You cannot do everything yourself. Covers the professional support network every Swiss business owner needs: Treuhand / fiduciaire (fiduciary/bookkeeping services), Steuerberater / conseiller fiscal (tax advisors), Rechtsanwalt / avocat (lawyers), and Berater / consultant (business consultants). Explains typical costs, how to choose providers, and what you can safely handle yourself versus what requires professional help.

**Key concepts:** Treuhand / fiduciaire, Steuerberater / conseiller fiscal, Rechtsanwalt / avocat, Berater / consultant, Mandat / mandat, Honorar / honoraires, Selbstbuchhaltung / tenue de comptabilité en propre

### 24. The Entrepreneur Mindset — Building for the Long Term

Closes the certification. Ties together all threads: the technical skills that got you here, the business skills you have now learned, and the mindset required for long-term success. Covers resilience / résilience, continuous learning / apprentissage continu, networking / réseautage, work-life balance / équilibre vie-travail, and the reality that business ownership is a marathon, not a sprint. Leaves candidates with a clear picture of what it takes to succeed as a Swiss entrepreneur / entrepreneur suisse.

**Key concepts:** Unternehmergeist / esprit d'entreprise, Resilienz / résilience, lebenslanges Lernen / apprentissage tout au long de la vie, Netzwerken / réseautage, Work-Life-Balance / équilibre vie-travail, Langfristigkeit / vision à long terme
